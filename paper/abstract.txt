Machine-learned band gaps are cheap enough to screen tens of thousands of candidate monolayers, but a number alone does not say when it should not be used. We describe NanoMatAI, an open tool that predicts the PBE band gap of a two-dimensional layer from its structure and attaches to every prediction a verdict (reliable, check or out-of-domain) and a calibrated 90% interval. A five-member CGCNN ensemble with a per-atom bond-angle descriptor, trained on 45 857 PBE monolayers from Alexandria, 2DMatPedia and MC2D, reaches a mean absolute error of 0.190 eV (95% CI 0.173-0.209) on a test set whose compositions never occur in training, against 0.430 eV for a composition model on the same set; on predictions it marks reliable the error is 0.10 eV. The verdict combines a five-seed metal/semiconductor gate, the ensemble spread and the cosine distance to the training set in the model's latent space; it orders the error on held-out data from four databases, including C2DB and JARVIS, on which the regressor never trained; on C2DB the error is 0.25 eV overall. Every change to the model was decided by a rule written before training and judged against a control retrained in the same session; seven of the last eight such experiments failed their rule and were not shipped. Auditing the data structure by structure found incorrect labels in Alexandria's planar honeycombs and 59 layers whose labels disagree between cells of the same structure, and measured the spin-orbit offset between databases. We also report eight errors of protocol made and corrected during the project, and a test of ten language models given the tool over the Model Context Protocol. Code, weights, data splits and a browser predictor that agrees with the Python package to within 1e-5 eV are public.
